Savoya quoting — flow diagrams

Three views of the same system: what the seller experiences, what actually runs in intake, and what runs in the document flow. The logic itself lives in decision-tree.md; these are drawn from it.

v0.27.5 · twelve-tool contract · refreshed 17 Oct 2026

4tools that can price a quote — all of them exact
4–11tool calls across intake
11–13questions for a full PDF + email
4–5questions if they stop at the quote

The version to show sales. No tools, no scripts — just the conversation.

a question for the seller something they receive a valid stopping point
Six questions on the document side, not five. A commission question was added on 10 October, after discount and before the presentation questions, so the numbers are settled first. It asks No, 1%, 5% or another percentage, states the rate on the document and in the email, and changes none of the numbers. There is still no fuel question — fuel is market-scoped and applied by the engine, so the document presents what Savoya charged rather than offering to change it. A CSM told to expect one will wait for a question that never comes.
One question after the quote, not two. "Happy with it?" and "Want a client document?" are a single question now (R93), and with several options it also settles which options go to the document, both first, and the seller is never asked how the product itself works. If a stop cannot be resolved, or a passenger range or a split group does not add up, the flow stops and asks for the missing piece instead of guessing (R83, R84, R91).
The nearby-market step is new, and it only appears when it is needed. An inventory check runs before every quote, silently. If the city covers the request, the seller never sees this branch at all. If it doesn't, they get the three nearest markets by driving distance and what each can add — and they choose. The system never picks a market for them.

The handoff is gated, and a gate failure is a file. handoff.py refuses to write when a stop is unresolved (R83), a passenger range has no headcount (R84), or a split group's counts do not sum to the headcount per trip (R91). It writes handoff/blocked.json with the gate, the reason and the fix, and the next skill reads it. The session asks the user for what is missing and runs the write again; it does not edit the handoff by hand.
The inventory check is unconditional, and that is the point. It runs before every exact quote even when the CSM declined the rate sheet, using the internal display so no rates are shown. If the primary market covers the request, the sourcing branch is skipped entirely and nobody is asked anything. It is an inventory check, not a driver or vendor schedule check — the counts are static fleet numbers, not date-specific availability.
Two things about sourcing that are not obvious from the shape of the graph. A sourced vehicle bills the primary market's rates, not its home market's — an Austin Mini Bus sourced into Dallas billed Dallas's $314.92/hr, not Austin's $244.09/hr, because the nearby market supplies the vehicle and not the price. And the returned counts are additional vehicles rather than totals, since any whose vendors already serve the reference market are excluded — a CSM reading "Market B · SUV 4" will otherwise promise a client four SUVs that may not be four extra ones. They are unconfirmed, too.
Never price a market you have not priced. A session did rate-sheet arithmetic and told a CSM that sourcing a Mini Bus from Austin would save about $80. The real quote came back $707.18 more expensive, because the arithmetic had missed the positioning drive time that sourcing exists to pay for. Choosing which candidates to price is fine; stating a price for one you have not priced is not, however hedged.
Three things in this graph reverse what the older diagrams showed. resolve_vehicle_types runs on every quote, because Core owns capacity and greeter eligibility and the static table was found wrong — it had Exec Van at "~10" where Core says 14, and omitted seven types including both greeters. A passenger is resolved only when the user names one, and no passenger id is cached. And G3 exists at all: a greeter is a priced Core unit, not a number a human types — a hand-entered "4 hrs at $235/hr = $940" went out against Core's own $404.20.
The one trap worth knowing before you use it. A temporary rate is a base rate: $250.00/hr supplied came back as $192.50/hr effective after a 30% contracted discount and 10% fuel. A CSM who enters the partner's cost quotes the client 23% below it, and afterwards it just looks like a price. Note also what is not on this graph any more — there is no approximate or estimated path at all. The tool that provided one was removed, because its discount arithmetic differed from the web app's.

Six base questions; greeter_cost is the conditional one. It is inserted only when a client question mentions a greeter and the market has no greeter rate. The script also drops cover and map for an email-only job, because nothing but the PDF and the printable document reads those answers. Pricing questions come before presentation ones, which is why commission sits at position 2 rather than at the end.
The commission amount is computed in code, on a fixed basis, and changes no total. The script works it out and the model never does (R59, R82). An earlier version of this page said it was never calculated; that is no longer true. No beneficiary is named, because the commission may be earned by the client or by an agency acting for them. It appears in the PDF heading block, the terms list and one flat email sentence; on No, nowhere at all.
The discount is a Core re-quote, not a document line. This reversed in 0.23: a percentage applied here was our arithmetic on Core's number, so it now goes back through calculate_temporary_quote_units as temporary_discount.basis_points and the document re-normalizes from the response. Discounts are additive in basis points — 30% contracted plus 10% here is 40%, not 37%. A document percentage line is refused outright.
The money check compares the engine's share, and a document line does not switch it off. It originally ran only when no document line had been added, on the reasoning that the total is then deliberately no longer the engine's — true of the total, not of the engine's share of it. One flat $200 coordinator line disabled the only fail-closed money check in the plugin, on a quote also built from a hand-transcribed engine response. Reproduced: a $100 slip in one leg's line total ships a client document $100 light, silently. A waived engine amount is added back before comparing, too — until that was fixed, --waive refused on every waiver and was unusable.
The two red boxes fail for opposite reasons, which is the point. The lower one catches a wrong total. The upper one catches a right total telling the wrong story: an additional discount with no line of its own stays inside Core's net, the fuel remainder swallows it, and the document prints a negative "Fuel surcharge" at a positive percentage. That shipped to a client. Nothing failed, because a reconciliation check structurally cannot catch a mislabel — so a separate guard now checks each line's sign against its own stated percentage.
The capture goes up twice, and a failed upload asks rather than mentions. A snapshot on the render pass and the full capture after the feedback exchange, under different titles, because Drive's create always creates and because a session that ends at delivery must still leave a record. It is always compressed — about 15× — so there is no size at which a capture stays behind; the old 25 KB ceiling meant the richest sessions were exactly the ones that never arrived. And when the upload fails, the user is the only one who can switch the connector on, so the flow asks them while the session is still alive. Three sessions in a row ended with the record stranded before that changed.
~ is not the user's computer. On claude.ai the home directory is a cloud container reclaimed with the session; on a linked desktop the shell's home is an isolated VM. Only a connected folder is really their disk. Two real captures — 192 KB and 36 KB — were written there, reported as saved, and lost. The capture now reports whether its local copy is durable, and when it isn't, Drive is the only destination and the upload is not optional.
