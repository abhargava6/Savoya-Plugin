# Email voice — from Robyn's and Julia's real emails

`samples/` holds 15 Savoya-authored emails (Robyn Schimmels, VP Sales; Julia Dobbins, CSM) from four real client threads. **Read two or three before drafting** — one Robyn, one Julia quote email — and match their rhythm. The guide below is distilled from them, not invented.

## Two registers, one house
- **Sales (Robyn):** relationship-first. Opens by thanking for the conversation or the opportunity. Recommends with a reason. Closes with a standing-by line. Signs "Warm Regards, Robyn".
- **CSM (Julia):** operational. Opens by thanking for the details. Presents the quote in the house block format. Explains policy once, plainly. Signs "All the best,".
Pick by the account manager's role; default to the CSM register for a quote email, the Sales register for a first-contact or follow-up.

## What the samples actually do
- **Exclamation marks are normal.** 2–5 per email, in the warm sentences — never inside the numbers block. "Thank you so much for thinking of us!" / "It was so nice to chat with you!" / "As always, let us know if you have any questions! We are happy to help."
- **They recommend, with the reason attached.** "With coach buses having a six-hour minimum and the added drive time to and from Vancouver, keeping one bus onsite for the whole day would definitely make the most sense." / "I'd recommend our MPV… perfect for a group of 4 with 3 teens and even has room for an extra bag or two."
- **They explain the pricing model when it matters, in one breath.** Hourly vs. transfer, what a minimum means ("simply the minimum charge requirement for the vehicle on that date"), garage-to-garage, what's included ("inclusive of federal taxes, fuel surcharges, and gratuity. Any incidental fees… passed along at cost").
- **Special events get named.** "This trip is taking place during the Formula 1 Grand Prix race event. We anticipate limited availability and there are also special rates in effect on this date."
- **Discounts are said out loud, warmly.** "All Onvia owners receive a 20% discount off of Savoya's services (hooray!) and I've incorporated the discount into the pricing below." / "which include a 30% discount as Robyn previously noted".
- **Bad news is delivered with the fix in the same paragraph.** "…cannot drop off directly at the golf course due to the narrow driveway. However, I called the Fairmont directly, and they confirmed…"
- **Sentences are short. Paragraphs are 1–3 sentences.** Blank line between every paragraph.

## The house quote block (per leg — this is what goes in the email body)
```
Scenario #1 – 1 coach bus from 9:00am–3:30pm
Vehicle type: Coach bus (max capacity: 55 passengers)
Cancellation policy*: 168 hours (1 week)
Minimum hours: 6 hours
Quoted time: 8.25 hours
Adjusted Client Hourly Rate: $539.28
Estimated Total: $5,072.60
```
Then, once, under the blocks:
`*Please note, this cancellation policy means this reservation is billable once 168 hours prior to the scheduled pickup time is reached. Modifications or cancellations made within this window may result in fees.`
The email is self-sufficient — the client can decide from the email alone. The PDF is the polished version, not the only version.

## Phrases in circulation
Openers: "Thank you so much for thinking of us!" · "It was so nice to chat with you today!" · "Thanks so much again for the opportunity to serve you in June!" · "Thank you for sending these details over for June 9th!" · "Thank you for your patience as we worked to quote out your needs in Bangkok."
Closers: "As always, let us know if you have any questions! We are happy to help." · "We'll be standing by at your service!" · "We will be sure we take excellent care of you!" · "Let me know the best time for you and we will make it happen!"
Sign-offs: "Warm Regards, Robyn" · "All the best, Julia"

## What they don't do
No "please find attached" as an opener. No "seamless", "elevate", "leverage". No bullet lists in the prose — the quote block is the only structured part. No hedging on numbers. Emoji appear in Robyn's emails occasionally; don't generate them.
